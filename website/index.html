01 THE PROJECT

When communication is limited,
preparation matters.

Long-distance missions can involve communication delays or periods without contact. Doctor AI demonstrates how an onboard tool can use a crew member’s questionnaire answers and medical references to suggest possible diagnoses and practical next steps while they wait to connect with mission control.

The prototype runs its language model locally, bringing the guided questionnaire, reference context, and generated assessment together in one working flow.

THE DESIGN CHALLENGE

Care changes when
Earth is far away.

On a long-duration mission, the crew may need to act before they can speak with a doctor on Earth. Doctor AI explores how a local tool could help a crew member organize symptoms and review possible next steps during that gap.

The project is designed around the person using it: a non-specialist who may be tired, under pressure, and working with limited onboard resources.

Communication
Long delays or contact blackouts can interrupt real-time support.
People
The interface should guide a crew member without assuming medical training.
Resources
An onboard tool must be useful without depending on a cloud connection.
PROJECT STATUS

What exists today.

The complete guided assessment flow is working and was presented at NASA HUNCH PDR. The next iteration will narrow its medical scope.

WORKING PROTOTYPE

Local LLM assessment

The end-to-end flow runs a local language model with questionnaire answers, system instructions, and medical-reference excerpts to generate a possible assessment and next steps.

NEXT ITERATION

Focus on one body system

PDR judges encouraged us to focus the broad concept on a single body system. Choosing that focused scope is a key next step.

What comes next: focus the assessment on one body system, refine the question flow, and keep improving response time and the interface.
